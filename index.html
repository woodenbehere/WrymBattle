How to play

When a hero's gold gauge fills, their menu opens. Tap any glowing name to switch who acts. Wait pauses the gauges while you choose; Active doesn't.

The back row halves physical damage dealt and taken. Magic ignores rows.

Options: open the menu (Party) and choose Options to switch between manual and auto battle, pick the auto strategy, choose which heroes fight on auto and their habits, set Wait or Active, turn random encounters and the mini map on or off, and switch between the Classic and Touch interfaces. Your battle settings are remembered.

Touch view: the game starts in touch view, which hides the menus and fills the screen with the game (switch to Classic in Options or the action ring). Hold anywhere on the left side and drag to walk with the thumb stick; tap the right side for a ring of actions (Party, Journal and whatever you can do where you are, plus Full screen, Help and Classic view to switch back). In battle, the hero whose turn it is gets a ring of commands (or just tap an enemy to Fight it); Magic, Skill and Item lists open as a second ring around the hero (two loops when long, with More › to page); to pick who a spell or item is for, just tap that enemy or hero. With a single-target attack spell, triple-tap empty space to spread it across every enemy, splitting the damage between them. Tap another glowing hero to switch who acts; Wait/Active and Auto are in the top-right corner. Bars under each hero show HP (green), MP (blue), Fury (pink) and the turn gauge (gold).

The pink bar is the limit gauge. It fills as a hero takes damage; when full, a Fury command appears.

After the dragon falls, the world map opens. Walk with the arrow keys or WASD, or press and hold on the map. Random battles happen as you walk; Camp restores HP and MP, and Run escapes most fights.

Frostfang Peak: a cave in the great mountain range leads up three floors to the summit, where Frostfang, the Rime Dragon, waits. Glowing save circles are safe spots: they save your progress, let you pitch a Tent, and are where you wake if the party falls. Help the miner, the stranded climber and the hermit on the way up.

The hidden valley: high in the island’s great mountain range lies a valley no road can reach. Fly the Aurora there to find Castle Arcanum and the village of Highmere, with the strongest spells, items and gear in the realm. The magi can raise the lost Isle of Aether, but a wall of storm clouds surrounds it and hurls back the airship. King Aldous can build you a port east of his castle: walk out along the pier to board the ship. On the main island she can only tie up at that pier; on the isle she can land on any beach. The Tower of Aether has a hundred floors, guardians on floors 25, 50 and 75, waystones every tenth floor (tap one three times until it blazes purple to return to the first floor), and Zephyrion the Sky Dragon at the top.

Day and night: a day passes in about six minutes. The icon in the top-left corner shows the sun by day, the moon and its phase by night (a 15-day cycle), and the weather; tap it for details. Weather drifts between clear, cloudy, rain and storms, with cloud shadows sweeping the ground. At night every area’s monsters are replaced by night forms about twice as tough, and new night-only creatures prowl: wraiths, gargoyles, moon moths and big cats (panthers, tigers, lions, lynxes, sabertooths and jaguars). Night monsters have MP and cast spells like Flame, Frost, Drain, Poison and Mend; once their MP is gone they can only fight, and Mana Siphon drains it. Rain brings Rain Toads, storms bring Thunderbirds, and Thornhares sleep at night. Every other full moon rises as a Blood Moon (the first on day 9, then every 30 days): zombies, ghouls and Zombie Knights swarm the wilds, Valemont’s gate and Thornwick’s gates shut until dawn, and the magi of Arcanum hold a holy barrier over their valley. In the week before, any king offers the Blood Moon Hunt: slay 5, 15, 30 or 50 of the dead for rising rewards. Inns, beds, tents and bunks let you sleep until morning or until nightfall, but no one sleeps under a Blood Moon. Shops close at dusk (19:00) and open at dawn (6:00), and townsfolk head home for the night. Monsters attack ships at sea: sea serpents, giant crabs, harpies and sahagin by day; krakens, ghost pirates, abyssal anglers and sirens by night. They’re manageable near the Eastern Port pier and grow far deadlier the farther out you sail. Fish bite more in the rain and around the full moon. Thieves’ guild doormen want the secret sign every time; get it wrong and you’re thrown out. The far isles: sail to Coralhaven on the Coral Reach (southwest) or Stormhaven on the Storm Isles (northeast), where the ship can land on any beach. Coralhaven sits on the east shore of its isle, beside its own pier: steer into the pier to tie up, and the town gate is just ashore. Each town has weapon, armor, item and magic shops, an inn, a guild board and a hidden thieves’ guild. The monsters there are the deadliest in the realm. Maelstrom, the Water Dragon, sleeps in a whirlpool in the southeast sea; sail into it to fight. Until it falls, a wall of seawater seals the Tower of Aether. Seasons: every three moons (45 days) the season turns: Fall, Winter, Spring, Summer. Winter brings snow instead of rain. The Homestead, a few leagues north of Castle Valemont, is yours to farm: stand on a plot to till, plant, water and harvest (or work the whole field at once). Crops grow only on days they’re watered or it rains, each has its season, and out-of-season crops wither. Old Hob sells seeds, animals and buildings (coop, barn, well, scarecrow, more fields, greenhouse, greenhouse well). Step into the farmhouse to rest and decorate: browse the catalog, mail an order from the red mailbox, and the furniture arrives in boxes by the door the next morning. Carry them in, then face a spot and choose Place furniture, or let Auto-arrange lay everything out. The Journal’s Homestead tab tracks harvest dates, animals, earnings and more. Walk into the greenhouse to farm its 288 beds, where any seed grows in any season and nothing withers; drop produce, eggs, milk and wool in the shipping bin to sell. Rows: in the main menu, double-tap a hero to move them between the front row (portrait on the left) and the back row (portrait shifted right). Back-row heroes take and deal half physical damage; in battle, use the Row command. Class advancement: at level 20, once the Hill Ogre is slain, King Aldous offers Garrick the Oath of Light, a trial against the Black Knight, to become a Paladin. Once the isle is raised, Queen Elowen of Arcanum holds the Rite of Mastery: Senna becomes a Master Summoner (Drakonis, level 20). Trials can’t hurt you: win or lose, the party is restored. Ilse is called instead: once she knows Grand Mend and reaches level 20, stay a night at the Coralhaven inn. She wakes alone in the town’s new church, the Chapel of the Tide (its tabernacle stands behind the altar, its red sanctuary lamp always lit; by day a line waits in the back pew for the confessional, and touching the holy water font beside the door fully restores the party; Our Lady’s statue stands in a rose garden outside), where the Seraph of the Tide explains a High Cleric’s duties and asks her five questions of conscience. Answer as a true healer would and she becomes a High Cleric (Benediction); miss one and she wakes at the inn, free to try again another night. Morrow becomes a Archmage another way: fly the airship over the chasm near Stormhaven and choose Descend to land in Gloamhollow, an underground city of sorcerers with hat-shaped houses (and no thieves’ guild). Its shops sell wood weapons, cloth armor, and two spells any magic user can learn: Mana Siphon (drain MP from an enemy) and Wall (each ally reflects the next three spells back at the caster). Only magic users may pass the Gate of the Depths, wearing no metal armor (crowns and helms included); Garrick and Vesh wait in town. The Depths run twenty floors, each harder than the last, with rune circles every five floors to return to town. Three Thrift Ring rings lie in chests on floors 6, 12 and 18: with one on, every spell costs 1 MP. King Nocturne waits on floor 20, nearly all spells and very hard to beat; Wall is the key. Beat him and Morrow becomes a Archmage (Oblivion), and you win the Night King’s Hat. Each promotion brings new gear, stronger stats and a signature skill: Holy Blade and Lay on Hands, Benediction, Oblivion or Bastion. Vesh wears a ninja outfit once he becomes a Ninja. The Journal keeps your stats, a bestiary, items collected and used, the Fish Book and your own notes. Save holds three games: save to any slot, load one, or start a new game in an empty slot. The game also saves itself to the slot you’re playing. On the world map, Full view hides the menus and zooms the map out (2×, 3× or 1.5×) so you see more of the world while you travel. Aboard the Aurora, choose Explore the ship to walk her decks: bunks below restore HP and MP, Quartermaster Hilde runs a shop, and the training room’s Battle Simulator lets you practice against any creature you’ve fought. Simulations give no EXP, gold or items, and the party is always restored to how it started, win or lose. Take the helm to fly on. Rest at an inn after dark and Vesh can slip out alone. At night he can’t leave town, but he can pick locks (tap when the pick is in the gold), sneak through sleeping houses, and steal goods to sell to the hidden thieves’ guild in every town (look for a trapdoor at night). Walking near sleepers wakes them; guards with lanterns catch anyone who gets too close. Guild masters give quests, the trainer teaches new battle skills, and the last quest makes Vesh a Ninja.

Fishing: while sailing, schools of fish ripple on the water. Sail up to one and choose Fish. Tap the water to cast, hold Reel to work the lure, keep still while a fish nibbles, then tap HOOK! the moment it bites. While landing it, watch the line: ease off when the fish thrashes or the line snaps. Snag chests on the seabed for treasure, including magic armor found nowhere else. Old Gill at the Adventurers’ Guild buys your catch, and the Fish Book logs every kind with its largest size and date.

Status ailments: some monsters poison (HP drains over time, even while walking), petrify (the hero is out of the fight until cured) or confuse (the hero attacks at random). Cure them with Antidotes, Stonebalm, Remedies, or the Heal spell. Confusion wears off when a battle ends; poison and stone last until cured, or until you rest at an inn or camp. Open Party outside battle to cast healing magic (Mend, Heal, Revive, Exit and more) and use items. A Warding Sash blocks them all. Exit warps the party out of any dungeon, castle or town to the world map. In Ironhold, the Rune Hall sells Firestorm, Blight and the summons Pyralis, Gaian and Drakonis, and the Training Hall teaches the Fighter monk arts and the Thief new tricks.

Deepdelve Mine: on the northern cape, sealed until you finish King Aldous’s Hill Ogre quest and defeat Frostfang. Then a mine shaft drops five levels to Ironhold, the dwarf city. Each level has a lift near its entrance that runs to any level you’ve already reached. Read the old tablets on the Sky Road, slay Gravemaw the Earth Dragon to free the trapped dwarves, and their King and Queen can blast free the airship Aurora. Walk aboard to fly anywhere on the world map with no random battles; choose Land to set down on open ground.

Castle Valemont: walk in to meet the King and Queen, rest in the guest rooms, raid the armory, and earn your way into the royal vault. Walk into people to talk to them.

Auto hands chosen heroes to the AI with a strategy you pick.

Keyboard: arrows to move, Enter to choose, Esc to go back.

Towns: walk into Valemont Village (inside the castle walls, on both sides of the road) or Thornwick. Walk around, talk to townsfolk, and walk into shop doors to shop or rest at the inn. The Adventurers’ Guild in Valemont Village posts bounties for gold, EXP and gear. Monsters give EXP and Gold, and heroes level up as they fight. Progress saves automatically once the dragon falls.

Heroes on auto · habit

A habit overrides the strategy whenever it can be used, using the strongest version of a spell the hero has learned. New spells appear here once bought. Full fury still fire.

Cast attack spells on all enemies

Like triple-tapping empty space: a single-target attack spell hits every enemy, with its damage split between them. Healing and support spells are not affected.
